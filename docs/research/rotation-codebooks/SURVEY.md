# Rotations, learned codebooks, and second-order compensation for LLM weight compression

**Research survey — 3 August 2026**

## Executive answer

There is room for rotations on top of learned codebooks, but the size of that room is not established for the system of interest. Rotations and codebooks solve different problems: a codebook chooses reproduction points, while an orthogonal change of basis changes the distribution seen by a finite-dimensional product partition and changes how the Hessian's correlations align with that partition. A sufficiently flexible, per-tensor learned codebook can plausibly absorb much of the *marginal-distribution* benefit of incoherence, but it cannot in general eliminate cross-coordinate Hessian structure, unfavorable product-block boundaries, or a hardware-grid constraint. This is an evidence-grounded inference from the mechanisms in [QuIP](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/0df38cd13520747e1e64e5b123a78ef8-Abstract-Conference.html), [QuIP#](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/tseng24a.html), [QTIP](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/6de2e84b8da47bb2eb5e2ac96c63d2b0-Abstract-Conference.html), and [AQLM](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/egiazarian24a.html), not a direct ablation of the target design. **No measured evidence was found** that holds a per-tensor learned, FP-grid-constrained product codebook fixed in capacity and compares rotation on versus off at 2–3 bits per quantizable weight. [Evidence quality: peer-reviewed full texts; inference explicitly identified.]

GPTQ/LDLQ-style compensation has the stronger case. Diagonal activation weighting and sequential compensation are not substitutes: the former changes the cost of each error using diagonal sensitivity, while the latter uses off-diagonal Hessian information to move the effect of already committed error into unquantized coordinates. [VPTQ](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.467/) supplies the most relevant controlled result: at 2.02 bpw on Llama-2-13B, disabling its channel-independent second-order update while retaining the learned VQ setup worsened WikiText-2 perplexity from 6.29 to 7.25 and C4 from 8.29 to 9.80. [Evidence quality: peer-reviewed full text; direct ablation.]

For the stated fail-closed native-kernel serving lane, the best next experiment is therefore to add sequential error feedback *inside the existing codebook assignment*, with no format or runtime change. The minimal decisive study is a 2×2 factorial—rotation off/on × compensation off/on—in which every arm relearns the same-capacity, same-grid codebooks and uses identical diagonal weighting, scales, calibration tokens, bit accounting, and assignment order. The rotation arm must be restricted to a demonstrably offline-fusible shared basis. [Evidence quality: recommendation derived from the direct [VPTQ](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.467/) ablation, the smaller residual compensation gap after incoherence in [QuIP](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/0df38cd13520747e1e64e5b123a78ef8-Abstract-Conference.html), and the absence of a target-matched rotation ablation; see Sections 5 and 7.]

## Scope, terminology, and evidence discipline

This survey concerns post-training **weight** compression at roughly 2–3 bits per quantizable weight, particularly MoE linears encoded by per-tensor learned product codebooks whose decoded entries lie exactly on NVFP4/FP8 grids. “Diagonal weighting” means a loss of the form \(\sum_j H_{jj}\lVert w_j-\hat w_j\rVert^2\), usually estimated from activation second moments (“imatrix”). “Compensation” means GPTQ/OPTQ/LDLQ-style sequential updates using off-diagonal information. “Rotation” includes random or learned orthogonal changes of basis, but the serving conclusions distinguish offline-fusible changes from online Hadamard or affine transforms. [Evidence quality: definitions used by this survey, aligned with [QuIP](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/0df38cd13520747e1e64e5b123a78ef8-Abstract-Conference.html), [GPTQ](https://openreview.net/forum?id=tcbBPnfwxS), and the [llama.cpp imatrix derivation](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/5263).]

Evidence labels mean:

- **Peer-reviewed, full text:** archival conference paper inspected beyond the abstract.
- **Preprint, full text:** manuscript inspected, but not treated as peer-reviewed.
- **Repository / official documentation:** useful for what software currently exposes or ships, not for causal quality claims.
- **Community:** maintainer/user discussion; useful operational evidence, weakest tier here.
- **Cross-paper comparison:** hypothesis-generating only unless calibration, model revision, bit accounting, and evaluation are matched.

The search prioritized papers, official proceedings, author repositories, and official product documentation. References that could not be verified were omitted. None of the central sources below is “abstract only”; where an abstract page is linked, the linked proceedings record also exposes the full paper. The inspected source list and evidence grades are recorded in the [bibliography](./bibliography.json). [Evidence quality: survey methodology and source audit.]

## 1. Rotation × fixed-lattice codebooks: QuIP → QuIP# → QTIP

### 1.1 QuIP: why incoherence precedes the codebook lineage

QuIP combines two separable ideas: **LDLQ**, an adaptive rounding rule equivalent to the OPTQ/GPTQ solution under the paper's quadratic proxy, and **incoherence processing**, random orthogonal/Kronecker transforms that spread large weights and Hessian eigenvectors. Its theory gives expected proxy loss proportional to \(\operatorname{tr}(D)\) for LDLQ versus \(\operatorname{tr}(H)\) for nearest rounding, where \(H=LDL^T\); the paper reports \(\operatorname{tr}(D)/\operatorname{tr}(H)\leq 0.65\) for tested OPT models. Thus rotation targets outliers/coherence, while LDLQ targets off-diagonal sensitivity. [Source: [QuIP paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/0df38cd13520747e1e64e5b123a78ef8-Abstract-Conference.html). Evidence quality: peer-reviewed, full text; theorem plus empirical Hessian statistic.]

The low-bit effect is large. On Llama-2-70B, the paper reports WikiText-2/C4 perplexity of 123.91/70.54 for 2-bit OPTQ versus 6.33/8.94 for QuIP; at 3 bits the comparison is 4.91/7.10 versus 3.85/6.14, and at 4 bits 3.60/5.91 versus 3.53/5.87. These are complete-method comparisons, so they do not assign the gain solely to rotation. [Source: [QuIP paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/0df38cd13520747e1e64e5b123a78ef8-Abstract-Conference.html), Llama-2 results table. Evidence quality: peer-reviewed, full text; matched table within one paper.]

An OPT-30B 2-bit ablation is more diagnostic:

| Method | WikiText-2 | PTB | C4 | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Nearest | 41,547.8 | 34,348.6 | 24,815.7 | Plain scalar nearest rounding collapses. |
| Nearest + incoherence | 12.04 | 18.12 | 14.11 | Rotation alone rescues nearest rounding. |
| LDLQ-RG | 49.40 | 73.45 | 29.12 | Compensation without incoherence remains poor. |
| LDLQ-RG + incoherence | 11.68 | 16.94 | 13.44 | The mechanisms compose. |
| QuIP/LDLQ + incoherence | 11.48 | 17.40 | 13.55 | Full method. |

[Source for all rows: [QuIP paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/0df38cd13520747e1e64e5b123a78ef8-Abstract-Conference.html), OPT-30B ablation. Evidence quality: peer-reviewed, full text; direct within-paper ablation.]

After incoherence, nearest rounding is only about 4–5% worse in perplexity than full QuIP on the reported WikiText-2 and C4 numbers (12.04 versus 11.48; 14.11 versus 13.55). That does **not** prove compensation is generally unimportant; it shows that this rotation made scalar decisions much better conditioned in this model/setting. [Source: same [QuIP ablation](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/0df38cd13520747e1e64e5b123a78ef8-Abstract-Conference.html). Evidence quality: peer-reviewed, full text; arithmetic on a direct ablation.]

### 1.2 QuIP#: incoherence as source shaping for a universal lattice

QuIP# replaces weaker Kronecker preprocessing with randomized Hadamard transforms (RHTs), arguing that transformed weight coordinates become approximately Gaussian and that the transformation is both stronger and cheaper. It then quantizes 8-dimensional blocks with **E8P**, a fixed, symmetric subset of the E8 lattice, and uses BlockLDLQ to propagate vector quantization error. The codebook is universal rather than learned per tensor, so source shaping is central: a nearly spherical Gaussian is precisely the distribution for which a fixed high-dimensional lattice has a clean, reusable geometry. [Source: [QuIP# paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/tseng24a.html). Evidence quality: peer-reviewed, full text; method description and stated motivation.]

QuIP#'s Llama-2 ablations quantify code geometry *after rotation*, but do not isolate rotation itself:

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 7B, 2 bit, Wiki/C4 | 6.19 / 8.16 | 8.22 / 11.0 | 11.2 / 14.5 | 6.93 / 8.84 at 2.02 bpw |
| 13B, 2 bit, Wiki/C4 | 5.35 / 7.20 | 6.06 / 8.07 | 7.04 / 9.37 | 5.70 / 7.59 at 1.97 bpw |
| 70B, 2 bit, Wiki/C4 | 3.91 / 5.71 | 4.16 / 6.01 | 4.58 / 6.51 | 3.94 / 5.72 at 2.07 bpw |
| 7B, 3 bit, Wiki/C4 | 5.41 / 7.04 | 5.60 / 7.34 | 5.77 / 7.61 | — |

[Source for all rows: [QuIP# paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/tseng24a.html), principal and ablation tables. Evidence quality: peer-reviewed, full text; within-paper evaluation. “No E8” replaces the lattice path with scalar quantization in the no-fine-tuning ablation, so the full-to-no-E8 gap also contains fine-tuning and must not be called a pure lattice delta.]

At Llama-2-70B 2-bit without fine-tuning, QuIP# additionally reports WikiText-2/C4 perplexity of 4.156/6.535 for E8P, 4.408/6.797 for D4, and 4.452/6.925 for an 8-dimensional Gaussian k-means codebook at the same nominal 2-bit rate; a larger 2.37-bpw E8 lattice reaches 3.702/6.082. This supports the value of E8P under the common RHT/BlockLDLQ pipeline. It does **not** test a per-tensor, activation-weighted learned codebook: the k-means comparator is trained as a universal Gaussian source code. [Source: [QuIP# paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/tseng24a.html), codebook ablation. Evidence quality: peer-reviewed, full text; direct codebook comparison with the stated scope limitation.]

QuIP# also makes the storage/serving trade explicit: its E8P table is about 1 KiB, whereas the paper characterizes an AQLM-style \(2^{16}\times8\) FP16 codebook as roughly 1 MiB. The author implementation uses custom CUDA and `fast-hadamard-transform`; its reported 2-bit throughput is 176 tokens/s for Llama-2-7B and 21.9 tokens/s for 70B, versus 81.1 and 8.72 for the compared AQLM kernels. These figures establish that the complete transform-aware implementation can be fast, not that its transforms are free in an arbitrary native GEMM serving lane. [Sources: [QuIP# paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/tseng24a.html) and [QuIP# repository](https://github.com/Cornell-RelaxML/quip-sharp). Evidence quality: peer-reviewed measured results plus repository implementation evidence.]

### 1.3 QTIP: trellis dimension after Gaussianization

QTIP retains RHT incoherence and BlockLDLQ, but replaces explicit small-dimensional lookup with trellis-coded quantization whose effective vector dimension can exceed 100. For an ideal Gaussian source at 2 bits/dimension, it reports normalized MSE 0.118 for scalar Lloyd-Max, 0.089 for 8D E8P, 0.069 for a 256D QTIP trellis, and a 0.063 rate-distortion limit. This is clean evidence that long-memory source coding improves a *Gaussianized fixed-source problem*; it is not evidence about an unrotated learned codebook. [Source: [QTIP paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/6de2e84b8da47bb2eb5e2ac96c63d2b0-Abstract-Conference.html). Evidence quality: peer-reviewed, full text; synthetic source-coding experiment.]

On Llama-2-7B at 2 bits without fine-tuning, increasing trellis length improves WikiText-2/C4 perplexity from 7.83/10.3 (L=8), through 7.49/9.67 (L=10) and 6.97/9.21 (L=12), to 6.82/8.96 (L=16), compared with QuIP# no-fine-tuning at 8.22/11.0. The complete fine-tuned hybrid QTIP reaches 5.86/7.73, versus QuIP# 6.19/8.16 and AQLM 6.14/8.09 in that table. [Source: [QTIP paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/6de2e84b8da47bb2eb5e2ac96c63d2b0-Abstract-Conference.html). Evidence quality: peer-reviewed, full text; within-paper ablation and comparator table.]

The hybrid QTIP decoder has trainable lookup values, but its trellis and incoherence assumptions remain structurally fixed; it is not the same intervention as relearning a compact product codebook independently for each tensor. QTIP reports 188/23.5 tokens/s on its 7B/70B setup, close to QuIP# at 186/22.2 and above its AQLM comparator at 81.5/8.78, using custom CUDA kernels. [Sources: [QTIP paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/6de2e84b8da47bb2eb5e2ac96c63d2b0-Abstract-Conference.html) and [QTIP repository](https://github.com/Cornell-RelaxML/qtip). Evidence quality: peer-reviewed full text plus repository.]

### Answer to Question 1

In this lineage, incoherence buys (1) suppression of individual weight outliers, (2) spreading of coherent Hessian eigenvectors, and (3) a near-isotropic Gaussian source that a universal E8/trellis code can cover efficiently. The evidence that this is decisive at 2 bits is strong for scalar and fixed/universal codes. The fraction attributable specifically to the codebooks being fixed cannot be recovered from the published ablations: neither [QuIP#](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/tseng24a.html), nor [QTIP](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/6de2e84b8da47bb2eb5e2ac96c63d2b0-Abstract-Conference.html) reports the required four-way comparison of fixed versus per-tensor learned codebook, each with rotation on versus off. **No measured evidence found for that interaction.** [Evidence quality: synthesis of the peer-reviewed [QuIP](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/0df38cd13520747e1e64e5b123a78ef8-Abstract-Conference.html)/QuIP#/QTIP ablations; missing interaction explicitly stated.]

## 2. Rotation × learned/adaptive codebooks

### What exists

**AQLM** learns additive codebooks for each linear layer, initializes them with residual k-means, and jointly refines codes/codebooks against activation-aware layer reconstruction before optional block fine-tuning. It does not use a rotation or GPTQ-style sequential error feedback. Its strong 2-bit results show that rotation is not *necessary* for competitive learned VQ; they do not measure the marginal value of adding rotation. Its layer-output objective can exploit the full calibration covariance during optimization, so calling it merely “diagonal imatrix” would also be inaccurate. [Sources: [AQLM paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/egiazarian24a.html) and [AQLM repository](https://github.com/vahe1994/AQLM). Evidence quality: peer-reviewed, full text; repository for shipped implementation.]

**AAAC** is a recent 4-bit learned-codebook example closer to the target's hardware-grid motivation: it learns two tiny activation-aware scalar codebooks per layer and encodes their selection without adding a normal index bit. It uses neither rotation nor sequential compensation and operates outside the 2–3-bpw regime, so it expands the adaptive-codebook landscape but supplies no rotation ablation. [Source: [AAAC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08692). Evidence quality: workshop paper/preprint, full text.]

**GPTVQ** learns VQ centroids and explicitly composes VQ assignment with GPTQ-style inverse-Hessian feedback, but it does not add rotation. **VPTQ** learns primary/residual centroids with Hessian-aware initialization and then propagates error column by column, again without rotation. These papers directly refute the idea that learned codebooks make off-diagonal compensation conceptually redundant, but neither answers whether a rotation still helps the same learned codebook. [Sources: [GPTVQ](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15319) and [VPTQ](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.467/). Evidence quality: preprint full text for GPTVQ; peer-reviewed full text for VPTQ.]

**QTIP's hybrid mode** fine-tunes lookup values inside a fixed trellis after RHT, and **QuIP#** compares E8P with a universal Gaussian k-means codebook under RHT. These are the closest lineage examples, but neither is a per-tensor learned-codebook rotation ablation. [Sources: [QTIP](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/6de2e84b8da47bb2eb5e2ac96c63d2b0-Abstract-Conference.html) and [QuIP#](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/tseng24a.html). Evidence quality: peer-reviewed, full text; method/ablation scope.]

Two newer results bound the question without matching the target:

- **BTC-LLM** combines a learned sub-1-bit binary codebook with a learned invertible Kronecker/diagonal affine transform. On Llama-2-7B at 0.8 bpw, removing the transform gives perplexity 9.23 and average accuracy 49.54; the Kronecker transform gives 6.95/55.64, and Kronecker plus diagonal gives 6.60/58.46. This is direct learned-codebook-plus-transform evidence, but the transform is nonorthogonal, operates far below the target rate, and requires an activation-side runtime transform rather than a fused-only weight rewrite. [Source: [BTC-LLM](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1066/). Evidence quality: peer-reviewed, full text; direct ablation but poor regime/serving match.]
- **QAM-W** uses deterministic block-Hadamard mixing, a universal two-dimensional Lloyd code trained on synthetic circular Gaussian data, and diagonal activation scaling. It explicitly leaves learned per-checkpoint codebooks and a learned-rotation iso-bpw comparison as future work. It is therefore evidence that the open question remains open, not evidence that learned codebooks obviate rotation. [Source: [QAM-W](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26339). Evidence quality: preprint, full text.]

### Mechanistic answer

A learned codebook can adapt centroid density to the rotated or unrotated marginal distribution. With enough codewords and training it may therefore capture much of the “Gaussian source matching” advantage that E8P/QTIP obtain from RHT. But a finite product codebook still assigns coordinates to blocks, a grid-constrained decoder restricts centroid locations, and a diagonal objective still ignores \(H_{ij}\). A rotation changes all three effective conditions: which coordinates share a block, how errors align with Hessian eigenvectors, and which rotated vectors are cheaply approximated by the permitted grid tuples. [Evidence quality: mathematical inference from the mechanisms documented in [QuIP](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/0df38cd13520747e1e64e5b123a78ef8-Abstract-Conference.html), [AQLM](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/egiazarian24a.html), and [QuIP#](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/tseng24a.html); not a measured result.]

For the target per-tensor FP-grid codebook, **no measured evidence found** for rotation-on versus rotation-off with all of codebook size, grid, scales, calibration set, assignment weighting, and effective bpw held fixed. Cross-paper [AQLM](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/egiazarian24a.html)-versus-[QuIP#](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/tseng24a.html)/[QTIP](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/6de2e84b8da47bb2eb5e2ac96c63d2b0-Abstract-Conference.html) rankings cannot answer the question because format overhead, fine-tuning, vector dimension, compensation, model handling, and kernels all differ. [Evidence quality: evidence-gap finding after primary-paper/repository review; cross-paper confounds documented in the linked method papers.]

## 3. Fusibility and runtime cost

### 3.1 Algebraic fusibility

For a linear map \(y=xW\), storing the residual stream in a shared orthogonal basis \(x'=xR\) lets the transformed weight be written \(W'=R^TW\). If the output is also kept in a shared basis, the output rotation can be folded into the same or the next projection. This makes a **global residual-stream rotation** offline-fusible when every branch meeting at a residual addition uses the same basis and normalization can be rewritten consistently. QuaRot uses this invariance to absorb its global rotation into weights and RMSNorm-related parameters. [Source: [QuaRot](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/b5b939436789f76f08b9d0da5e81af7c-Abstract-Conference.html). Evidence quality: peer-reviewed, full text; algebra and implementation.]

Fusibility fails wherever a general rotation would have to cross a coordinatewise nonlinear or position-dependent operation. In a gated MLP, an intermediate rotation cannot commute through SiLU and elementwise multiplication, so the transform before the down projection is online unless the architecture/kernel is changed. In attention, arbitrary head-dimension rotations do not commute with RoPE; QuaRot therefore applies a post-RoPE online Hadamard for Q/K and an online transform on value/head intermediates. Residual additions require all inputs in the same basis. These are structural constraints, not compiler limitations. [Source: [QuaRot](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/b5b939436789f76f08b9d0da5e81af7c-Abstract-Conference.html). Evidence quality: peer-reviewed, full text; architecture derivation.]

Normalization and attention layout narrow the admissible rotations further. RMSNorm is equivariant to an orthogonal basis change because it depends on vector norm, which is why the Llama-family derivations are clean. Ordinary LayerNorm also subtracts the coordinate mean; a general orthogonal matrix does not preserve that operation unless it preserves the all-ones direction, so arbitrary residual rotations cannot simply be carried through it. With MQA/GQA, shared K/V heads also rule out independent per-query-head rotations unless the rotations respect the sharing groups. These are algebraic constraints inferred from the architectures; the surveyed QuaRot/SpinQuant implementations validate selected Llama-family layouts, not every attention variant. [Sources: [QuaRot](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/b5b939436789f76f08b9d0da5e81af7c-Abstract-Conference.html) and [SpinQuant](https://openreview.net/forum?id=ogO6DGE6FZ). Evidence quality: peer-reviewed full texts for implemented architectures; LayerNorm and head-sharing statements are explicit algebraic inferences.]

SpinQuant makes the split explicit: learned \(R_1\) residual rotations and \(R_2\) value/output rotations are fused into weights, while fixed \(R_3\) attention and \(R_4\) feed-forward Hadamards remain online. Its reference implementation depends on the CUDA `fast-hadamard-transform` package. [Sources: [SpinQuant paper](https://openreview.net/forum?id=ogO6DGE6FZ) and [SpinQuant repository](https://github.com/facebookresearch/SpinQuant). Evidence quality: peer-reviewed, full text plus repository.]

### 3.2 MoE-specific constraints

A single shared residual-basis rotation can be folded into every expert's input projection and into the router weight, preserving router logits up to the same algebraic reparameterization. A distinct **per-expert input rotation** cannot be applied before routing because the selected expert is not yet known; applying it afterward requires an online expert-specific transform. Likewise, expert-intermediate rotations cannot cross the expert's activation/gating nonlinearity. These are algebraic inferences; **no measured MoE-specific rotation × learned-codebook ablation was found**. [Evidence quality: inference from the linear/reparameterization rules in [QuaRot](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/b5b939436789f76f08b9d0da5e81af7c-Abstract-Conference.html); evidence gap explicitly marked.]

A shared rotation can still change finite-precision routing behavior if the folded router weight is itself quantized: small router-logit errors can flip top-k choices and change expert load. A decisive MoE study therefore has to report route-flip rate and load balance, not only reconstruction error or perplexity. **No measured evidence found** in the surveyed codebook/rotation literature for this router-specific failure mode. [Evidence quality: numerical inference from the graph reparameterization used by [QuaRot](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/b5b939436789f76f08b9d0da5e81af7c-Abstract-Conference.html); no direct MoE measurement located.]

## 4. Rotations for activations: a separate axis

QuaRot and SpinQuant primarily motivate rotations by the outliers that make low-bit **activations and KV caches** hard to quantize. A weight-only learned codebook does not address dynamic activation outliers, so success or failure of a weight codebook says little about the value of activation rotations in W4A4/W4A8/KV4 serving. [Sources: [QuaRot](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/b5b939436789f76f08b9d0da5e81af7c-Abstract-Conference.html) and [SpinQuant](https://openreview.net/forum?id=ogO6DGE6FZ). Evidence quality: peer-reviewed, full text; stated motivation and quantization axis.]

QuaRot's weight-only table also shows composition with GPTQ. On Llama-2-70B at W4/W3/W2 A16, GPTQ reports WikiText-2 perplexity 3.87/5.91/25.30, while QuaRot+GPTQ reports 3.41/3.72/5.60. QuaRot+RTN reports 3.66/5.25/∞, illustrating both the large low-bit rotation benefit and the remaining value of compensation. These are scalar quantizers, not learned VQ. [Source: [QuaRot paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/b5b939436789f76f08b9d0da5e81af7c-Abstract-Conference.html), weight-only ablation. Evidence quality: peer-reviewed, full text; direct within-paper factorial-like comparison.]

SpinQuant's W4A4KV16 ablation finds a smaller but persistent GPTQ increment after rotation. For Llama-3-8B, SpinQuant-Hadamard with RTN/GPTQ gives average zero-shot accuracy 64.6/65.8 and perplexity 7.7/7.1; for Llama-2-7B it gives 61.8/64.1 and 6.1/5.9; for 13B, 65.8/67.2 and 5.4/5.2; for 70B, accuracy is 71.1/71.0 while perplexity is 3.9/3.8. [Source: [SpinQuant](https://openreview.net/forum?id=ogO6DGE6FZ), RTN/GPTQ ablation. Evidence quality: peer-reviewed, full text; direct ablation.]

Static fused rotations can improve residual-stream and weight distributions, so they are not useless for activation quantization. They cannot by themselves rotate tensors created *after* RoPE, SiLU/gating, or other nonlinear operations; that is why QuaRot's and SpinQuant's strongest W4A4 designs retain online transforms. The exact marginal gain of “fused rotations only” is architecture- and quantizer-dependent, and **no target-matched MoE learned-codebook ablation was found**. [Sources: [QuaRot](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/b5b939436789f76f08b9d0da5e81af7c-Abstract-Conference.html) and [SpinQuant](https://openreview.net/forum?id=ogO6DGE6FZ). Evidence quality: peer-reviewed mechanism evidence; final evidence gap explicit.]

## 5. GPTQ / LDLQ / sequential compensation versus diagonal weighting

### 5.1 Theory: different information, compatible objectives

For calibration activations \(X\), the usual layer proxy is \(\lVert(W-\hat W)X\rVert_F^2 = \operatorname{tr}((W-\hat W)H(W-\hat W)^T)\), with \(H=XX^T\). Diagonal imatrix weighting keeps only \(H_{jj}\). GPTQ greedily commits a column/block and uses an inverse-Hessian update to change the still-unquantized weights so their future error cancels the committed error under the full quadratic. LDLQ expresses the equivalent feedback through an \(LDL^T\) factorization; QuIP proves the equivalence to OPTQ/GPTQ for its setup. [Sources: [GPTQ](https://openreview.net/forum?id=tcbBPnfwxS) and [QuIP](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/0df38cd13520747e1e64e5b123a78ef8-Abstract-Conference.html). Evidence quality: peer-reviewed, full text; algorithms and theorem.]

If \(H\) is diagonal in the assignment basis, sequential compensation has nothing to exploit. If \(H\) has material off-diagonal entries, diagonal weighting cannot reproduce the error-redistribution update. Rotation may make \(H\) or the error distribution more benign, thereby reducing compensation's marginal value—as QuIP's rotated nearest-versus-LDLQ gap suggests—but it does not make the two objectives identical in general. [Source: [QuIP theory and ablation](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/0df38cd13520747e1e64e5b123a78ef8-Abstract-Conference.html). Evidence quality: peer-reviewed theorem plus evidence-grounded inference.]

### 5.2 Does compensation compose with VQ?

Yes. GPTVQ quantizes a vector of columns, assigns a centroid using a Hessian-derived metric, accumulates the vector quantization error, and applies the inverse-Hessian update to the remaining columns. The paper reports no meaningful difference between using the full within-vector sub-Hessian and its diagonal for the *local centroid assignment*, but that observation does not remove the subsequent cross-column GPTQ feedback. [Source: [GPTVQ](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15319). Evidence quality: preprint, full text; algorithm and reported assignment-metric observation.]

VPTQ's “channel-independent second-order optimization” similarly propagates quantization error while choosing learned vector centroids. Pyramid Vector Quantization combines a fixed spherical code, randomized Hadamard preprocessing, and GPTQ-style residual correction. Thus residual-feedback VQ is implemented in multiple papers, although only VPTQ provides the clean learned-VQ on/off ablation extracted below. [Sources: [VPTQ](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.467/) and [Pyramid Vector Quantization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.16926). Evidence quality: peer-reviewed full text for VPTQ; preprint full text for PVQ.]

### Answer to Question 5

At 2–3 bpw, the direct [VPTQ](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.467/) result is material evidence that off-diagonal compensation adds value to learned VQ even when Hessian-aware weighting/initialization already exists. [QuIP](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/0df38cd13520747e1e64e5b123a78ef8-Abstract-Conference.html) suggests that good incoherence can shrink that increment, while [QuaRot](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/b5b939436789f76f08b9d0da5e81af7c-Abstract-Conference.html) shows that rotation can still add dramatically after GPTQ on scalar grids. For the exact per-tensor learned FP-grid codebook, **no measured evidence found** comparing (a) diagonal-only assignment, (b) the same assignment plus sequential compensation, and (c) both plus a fused-only rotation. [Evidence quality: synthesis of the direct and confounded comparisons above; target-specific evidence gap explicit.]

## 7. Verdict for per-tensor learned, grid-constrained codebooks

### 7.1 Expected marginal value of fused-only rotations

**Verdict: plausible but uncertain; likely smaller than for fixed E8/trellis or scalar grids, and potentially concentrated in the lowest-rate/outlier tensors.** Learned per-tensor centroids should absorb some marginal source-shaping benefit, while the exact FP-grid constraint, product partition, and residual off-diagonal sensitivity leave mechanisms for a rotation to help. QuIP/QuaRot show enormous 2-bit benefits in scalar/fixed settings; BTC-LLM shows a learned codebook can still benefit from a transform, but no result matches the target's rate, grid, orthogonality, or fused-only constraint. **No measured evidence found for the exact intervention.** [Evidence quality: evidence-grounded inference from [QuIP](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/0df38cd13520747e1e64e5b123a78ef8-Abstract-Conference.html), [QuIP#](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/tseng24a.html), [AQLM](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/egiazarian24a.html), and [BTC-LLM](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1066/); uncertainty explicit.]

Only a **shared residual-stream rotation** should enter the first experiment. Per-expert or post-nonlinearity transforms would add kernels, complicate routing, and answer a different serving question. A fused rotation is not automatically free at quantization time: it is free only after an exact graph-level proof that every inverse transform is folded into adjacent weights and all residual/router paths remain functionally equivalent before quantization. [Source for fusibility constraints: [QuaRot](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/b5b939436789f76f08b9d0da5e81af7c-Abstract-Conference.html). Evidence quality: peer-reviewed mechanism plus target-specific engineering inference.]

### 7.2 Expected marginal value of GPTQ/LDLQ inside codebook assignment

**Verdict: medium-to-high probability of useful gain at 2–3 bpw, with no inference-time cost.** VPTQ's same-codebook-family 2.02-bpw ablation improves perplexity by 13–15%; GPTVQ supplies a concrete vector/block update; and LLM Compressor's 4-bit scalar example shows diagonal imatrix and GPTQ can be additive. QuIP cautions that the gain may become modest after a strong rotation and that order/damping matter. [Sources: [VPTQ](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.467/), [GPTVQ](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15319), [LLM Compressor imatrix example](https://docs.vllm.ai/projects/llm-compressor/en/latest/examples/imatrix/), and [QuIP](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/0df38cd13520747e1e64e5b123a78ef8-Abstract-Conference.html). Evidence quality: peer-reviewed direct ablation + preprint algorithm + official example; inference labeled.]

The safest integration is blockwise residual feedback around the existing assignment oracle: choose the grid-constrained codeword using the current diagonal activation-weighted objective, compute the committed error, update the unassigned columns using a damped/cholesky-stable Hessian factor, and periodically refresh the residual. This preserves the exported codebook, indices, scales, and native kernel contract; only offline quantization changes. GPTVQ establishes this composition pattern, though the precise update must be benchmarked for the target block layout. [Source: [GPTVQ](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15319). Evidence quality: preprint algorithm plus target-specific implementation recommendation.]

### 7.3 Better next experiment and minimal decisive design

**Do compensation first.** It has the closest direct learned-VQ evidence, is format-preserving, and costs nothing at serving. A fused-only rotation is second because its exact learned/grid-constrained benefit is an open empirical interaction and because graph-level fusibility is harder in MoE. [Evidence quality: recommendation based on the [VPTQ](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.467/) direct ablation and [QuaRot](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/b5b939436789f76f08b9d0da5e81af7c-Abstract-Conference.html) fusibility constraints.]

The decisive study should still be a factorial so the second question is not deferred indefinitely:

1. **Four arms:** no rotation/no compensation; no rotation/compensation; fused shared rotation/no compensation; fused shared rotation/compensation. Relearn every per-tensor codebook independently in every arm; never rotate an already-trained codebook. [Evidence quality: experimental-design recommendation addressing the interaction missing from [QuIP#](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/tseng24a.html), [QTIP](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/6de2e84b8da47bb2eb5e2ac96c63d2b0-Abstract-Conference.html), and [AQLM](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/egiazarian24a.html).]
2. **Hold constant:** exact FP grid, vector/block dimension, number of codewords, two-tier scale representation, outlier policy, quantizable-only bpw, calibration sequences/order, diagonal assignment weights, expert sampling, and fine-tuning budget (preferably none in the first pass). [Evidence quality: control design motivated by the confounded comparisons in [QuIP#](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/tseng24a.html) and [GPTVQ](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15319).]
3. **Rates/models:** at least 2.0, 2.5, and 3.0 effective bpw; one dense model for comparability and one representative MoE with enough experts to expose routing effects. Report multiple rotation seeds/sign patterns and confidence intervals. [Evidence quality: recommendation targeted to the stated operating range; rotation variability is a known practical concern in [SpinQuant](https://openreview.net/forum?id=ogO6DGE6FZ).]
4. **Metrics:** calibration reconstruction/KL, held-out full-token perplexity, downstream accuracy, quantizable-only bpw, codebook occupancy, FP-grid saturation, scale selection, quantization wall time/GPU utilization, and per-layer/expert error. For MoE also report top-k route flips, router-logit error, expert load distribution, and rare-expert quality. [Evidence quality: experiment recommendation grounded in the reconstruction/perplexity mismatch measured by [QuIP#](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/tseng24a.html); MoE routing metrics are inference-driven because no direct published ablation was found.]
5. **Fusibility gate:** prove float-model equivalence before quantization, enumerate every folded matrix, and reject any arm leaving an online transform. Then run the normal native-kernel export/serve validation unchanged. [Evidence quality: engineering gate derived from [QuaRot's](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/b5b939436789f76f08b9d0da5e81af7c-Abstract-Conference.html) transformation algebra and the target fail-closed contract.]

A useful early stopping rule is: if compensation improves held-out KL/perplexity consistently at fixed bpw without shifting codebook occupancy pathologically, implement it before exploring rotations. If rotation helps only when compensation is absent, it is primarily conditioning the greedy assignment; if it remains additive with compensation, it is changing the product/grid representation itself. That interaction, rather than either main effect alone, answers whether learned grid codebooks have already captured the incoherence benefit. [Evidence quality: experimental interpretation derived from the 2×2 design and the composition in [GPTVQ](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15319); not a prior measured claim.]

## Bottom line

- **Do learned codebooks obviate rotations?** Not in theory, and not demonstrated empirically. They likely absorb part of the fixed-codebook source-shaping gain. The exact remaining gain for per-tensor FP-grid codebooks is unmeasured. [Evidence quality: peer-reviewed mechanism evidence from [QuIP#](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/tseng24a.html) and [AQLM](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/egiazarian24a.html); target-specific evidence gap.]
- **Does GPTQ-style compensation add beyond diagonal imatrix weighting?** Yes in principle, because it uses off-diagonal curvature; and VPTQ measures a substantial 2.02-bpw learned-VQ gain. The exact gain in the target codebook is still to be measured. [Evidence quality: [QuIP](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/0df38cd13520747e1e64e5b123a78ef8-Abstract-Conference.html) theory + [VPTQ](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.467/) direct ablation, peer-reviewed full text.]
- **What next?** Implement offline sequential compensation first, then run the controlled fused-rotation factorial. Do not infer a native-serving result from research systems whose quality depends on online custom transforms. [Evidence quality: recommendation grounded in [VPTQ](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.467/) and the [QuIP# implementation](https://github.com/Cornell-RelaxML/quip-sharp).]
